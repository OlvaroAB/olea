# Grain Provenance and Clast Imbrication

## What sets a conglomerate's provenance signal?
The proportion of unstable minerals surviving in the bed. Feldspar breaks down quickly, so a feldspar-rich conglomerate records a short, fast delivery from the upland source; a quartzose one records a long or repeated one. Compare against [[Hummocky stratification]].

## Describe the stages of clast alignment in a rolling bedload
1. A rolling clast strikes an already-resting clast and stalls against it
2. The stalled clast tips until its long axis leans downstream ([[Imbrication]])
3. Following clasts stack against the same face, each leaning the same way
4. Winnowing removes the fine matrix from between the leaning clasts
5. The [[Paraconformity]] above may then remove all record of what came next

## What keeps an imbricated fabric from being re-set by the next flood?
	- The [[Paraconformity]] marks a pause long enough for the fabric to be cemented in place
	- Clasts locked against one another need more shear to dislodge than to deposit
	- Net result: the fabric records the last event strong enough to move the whole grain skeleton

## What is the practical difference between roundness and sphericity?
Roundness measures the sharpness of the corners; sphericity measures the overall shape. A flat, well-worn slab is highly rounded and poorly spherical at once — which is exactly why the two are logged as separate columns and never averaged into one.
