# Introduction to Clastic Sediment

## What is a clastic particle built from?
A clastic grain is a fragment of an older rock, released by weathering and moved by wind, water or ice before it comes to rest. Its history is written into three readable properties: what it is made of, how big it is, and how rounded it has become.

## What does a geologist record about a clastic accumulation?
- Grainsize — the calibre of the fragments, from silt to conglomerate
- Roundness — how far the corners have been worn away in transit
- Sphericity — how close the fragment sits to an equant shape
- Composition — quartz, feldspar and rock fragments in measurable proportion

## Why does the source of the grains matter?
The mineral mixture in a sandstone points back at the rock that supplied it, so a quartzose bed and a feldspar-rich bed of the same calibre record two different upstream stories. See [[Hummocky stratification]] and [[Bioturbation]].

## How does a loose accumulation turn into lithified strata?
A deposit is whatever a single event left behind. It only becomes a bed once burial and [[Cementation]] have turned the loose fragments into rock — and only then can [[Imbrication]] be read from a cut face rather than disturbed by the hammer.
