# Chorale No. 12 — Phrase One, Close Listening

## Who is carrying the harmonic argument in this phrase?
No single part — the chorale withholds a melodic lead entirely, and Phrase One trains the ear to read the harmony through what the inner parts avoid doing rather than what the top part states.

## What does the phrase's first chord pledge about the harmony?
Read that first chord as the phrase's public face: every progression the composer keeps diatonic for the listener is a progression where a chromatic decision has already been deferred to the second phrase.

## How does the first ascending fourth set up the whole phrase?
An ascending fourth answered by a descending step — the leap itself plants the imbalance Phrase One spends its remaining bars resolving.

## What is the function of the recurring stepwise descent?
Track every time an inner part falls by step rather than holding a common note — it is the chorale's shorthand for a dissonance about to be prepared. See [[Suspension]].
