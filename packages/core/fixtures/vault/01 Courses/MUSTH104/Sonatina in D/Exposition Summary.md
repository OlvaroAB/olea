# Sonatina in D — Exposition Summary

## How does the unaccompanied first subject carry meaning before the harmony moves?
The unaccompanied first subject (one line, nothing under it) is stated identically at the beginning and the end of the exposition — same register, same articulation — so any part that is doubled differently against it reads as changed.

## Which chord functions as the antithesis to the returning subdominant?
The chord that never resolves as expected functions as an [[Appoggiatura]] against it — every arrival the subdominant prepares gets an unprepared counterexample standing in the next bar.

## What harmonic choice signals the arrival of the second subject?
Nothing in the texture physically changes except the doubling and the register — a deliberate choice that puts the burden of "we have arrived somewhere new" entirely on harmony rather than on figuration.

## How is the four-part texture kept even?
No part gets an uninterrupted run longer than the others — the exposition distributes the moving line evenly, which is itself a claim that no single part is the "right" one to follow.
