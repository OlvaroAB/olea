# Halloran (2018) — Chorale Doubling in Keyboard Realisation

## What is "doubling" in the sense this paper means?
Not the keyboard practice of one hand playing two parts — Halloran means a texture-level doubling, where an inner part is written to shadow the outer part closely enough that the listener reads them as two statements of one line.

## How does this connect to unprepared dissonance?
Halloran treats the [[Appoggiatura]] as doubling's simplest case — displacement rather than shadowing — and argues most "ornaments" in late keyboard chorales are closer to doubled lines than to true decoration, which changes how an ornamented bar should be realised.

## Where does the consecutive-fifths argument come in?
When the doubling happens between the outer parts rather than across the inner ones, Halloran calls it "outer doubling" — his term for one flavour of [[Consecutive fifths]], where the listener senses the gap between the notated independence and the sounding one.
